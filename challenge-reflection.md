If we had 5 more minutes, we would strengthen the base even more to prevent the tilting. We would also add more support to the middle section to make the structure more stable. Additionally, we might try to make the top section more balanced to better support the marshmallow.


**What engineering principles did we discover? | 我们发现了什么工程原理？**

*For example: triangles are stable, base needs to be wide, weight distribution, etc.*  
*例如：三角形是稳定的、底座需要宽、重量分布等*

We discovered that triangles are very stable structures, which is why we used triangular columns and pyramids. We learned that a wide, firm foundation is crucial for supporting height - our three small foundations worked much better than a single column. We also realized that weight distribution matters - the structure needs to be balanced, and the marshmallow at the top needs proper support. Additionally, we found that multiple support points (like our three foundations) distribute the load better than a single point.

## 🎓 Lessons Learned | 学到的经验

### Key Takeaways | 关键收获

**The most important thing I learned from this challenge | 我从这个挑战中学到的最重要的事情**

The most important thing I learned is that testing and iteration are crucial. We started with one design, but when we tested it and saw it wasn't working well, we quickly adapted to the three-foundation approach. This taught me that it's okay to change plans when something isn't working, and that early testing saves time in the long run. Also, I learned that collaboration and listening to different ideas leads to better solutions.


**One thing I would definitely do differently next time | 下次我肯定会做得不同的一件事**

Next time, I would spend a bit more time planning the structure's stability from the beginning. I would think more carefully about how to prevent tilting and how to distribute the weight better. Also, I would test the foundation's stability more thoroughly before building higher, to catch problems earlier.

## 🌟 Maker Mindset Connection | Maker 思维的联系

### How the Challenge Embodies Maker Principles | 挑战如何体现 Maker 原则

**Curiosity | 好奇**: How did we explore different possibilities?  
我们如何探索不同的可能性？

We explored different possibilities by trying multiple design approaches. We started with a single column design, but when that didn't work well, we were curious about whether multiple foundations would be better. We tested the three-foundation idea and discovered it worked much better. We also explored different ways to build the top section, trying both triangular pyramids and other shapes to see what would support the marshmallow best.

**Hands-on | 动手**: How did building teach us more than just thinking?  
建造如何比仅仅思考教会我们更多？

Building taught us things we couldn't have learned just by thinking. For example, we thought a single column would work, but when we actually built it, we discovered it wasn't stable enough. The hands-on experience showed us how the spaghetti sticks actually behave under weight - they bend and need support. We learned about the real strength and limitations of our materials by actually working with them, not just imagining how they would work.

**Fail Fast | 快速试错**: How many times did we fail and try again?  
我们失败了多少次并再次尝试？

We failed and tried again 2-3 times. Our first design (single column) didn't work well, so we quickly abandoned it and tried the three-foundation approach. When parts of the structure started tilting, we adjusted and added more support. Each failure taught us something - we learned that we needed stronger foundations, better weight distribution, and more support points. By failing fast and learning quickly, we were able to improve our design within the time limit.

**Iteration | 迭代**: How did we improve our design step by step?  
我们如何一步步改进我们的设计？

We improved our design step by step through iteration. First, we built a simple single column design. When we tested it and found it wasn't stable, we iterated to use three foundations instead of one. Then we built the middle section, and when we noticed it was tilting, we added more support. Each iteration built on what we learned from the previous version, making small improvements until we had a structure that could stand and reach 110cm.

**Interdisciplinary | 跨学科**: What different skills did our team bring?  
我们的团队带来了哪些不同的技能？

Our team brought different perspectives and skills. One person might have been better at thinking about structural stability, while the other might have been better at quickly building and testing. We combined our ideas about geometry (triangles), physics (weight distribution and stability), and engineering (how to build something that stands). We also used communication skills to share ideas and problem-solving skills to overcome challenges together.

**Open Sharing | 开源分享**: Did we look at other teams' approaches?  
我们有观察其他团队的方法吗？

Yes, we did look at other teams' approaches during the challenge. We noticed that some teams were using different base designs, and we learned from seeing what worked and what didn't work for them. This helped us think about our own design and consider alternatives. The open sharing of ideas and approaches in the classroom environment helped everyone learn from each other.

My advice would be: don't spend too much time planning - start building and testing early, because you'll learn more from actually working with the materials than from just thinking.
